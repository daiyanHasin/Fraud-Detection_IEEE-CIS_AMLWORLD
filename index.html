Research report, 14 October 2026

Same evidence, different learners: a past-only evidence layer and a neural hybrid for fraud and money-laundering detection

Hasin Md. Daiyan

Evaluated on IEEE-CIS card fraud (590,540 transactions) and IBM AMLworld HI-Small money laundering (5,077,237 transactions)

Read the full write-up (PDF) IEEE-CIS notebook AMLworld notebook Cite

Abstract

Fraud and money-laundering models are usually compared on features that quietly use the future: random splits, labels that would not yet be known, or aggregates computed over the whole dataset. We build a past-only evidence layer of 87 features, each computed from earlier transactions only, in which a fraud label becomes usable only after a realistic confirmation delay (30 days for card chargebacks, 1 day on AMLworld). The same layer is given to LightGBM and to a fully neural hybrid of two component models: TabM+G, which reads what the transaction looks like, and a deviation model that attends over the same customer's last 16 transactions. Their logits are combined by a fixed weighted average.

Under a strictly chronological split with three seeds and paired bootstrap tests, the evidence layer is the dominant effect: it lifts LightGBM by +0.090 PR-AUC on IEEE-CIS and +0.468 on AMLworld, and periodic retraining never replaces it. Given the same evidence, the neural hybrid is slightly below LightGBM on card fraud (0.630 vs 0.635 PR-AUC) and ahead of it on money laundering on every seed (0.650 vs 0.625). It wins where customer history is rich, and it is the most stable model on both datasets. A per-transaction evidence gate, a separate graph model and TabNet are all reported as ablations that do not improve on the simpler design.

1The problem

A fraud model in production scores each transaction using only what is known at that moment. Most published comparisons on IEEE-CIS do not respect this, which makes their numbers optimistic and their model rankings unreliable.

Three practical problems motivate this work.

  1. Leakage through time. Random or stratified splits put a card's later transactions in training and its earlier ones in test. The Fraud Dataset Benchmark shows the cost: AutoGluon reaches 0.969 ROC-AUC in its own internal validation and 0.855 on an out-of-time test.
  2. Labels arrive late. A card fraud is typically confirmed through a chargeback weeks after the transaction. Features that use a customer's fraud history must therefore ignore labels younger than the confirmation delay, or they read information a bank would not have.
  3. Unfair model comparisons. Claims that a neural or graph model beats gradient-boosted trees usually give the two learners different information. The reproduced paper this project started from (Ahmad et al., 2026) reports 0.206 PR-AUC for a TabNet–GraphSAGE hybrid against 0.394 for LightGBM on IEEE-CIS. That gap says little about architecture when the inputs differ.

The study is organised around four research questions.

RQ1How much does past-only evidence add?

Entity memory with delayed labels lifts LightGBM by +0.090 PR-AUC on IEEE-CIS and +0.468 on AMLworld, better on every seed. Retraining LightGBM periodically is worse than training once.

RQ2Given identical evidence, can a neural model match trees?

On IEEE-CIS the hybrid is 0.006 below LightGBM+ (worse on two seeds, tied on one) with equal ROC-AUC. On AMLworld it is 0.025 ahead on every seed.

RQ3Where does each learner win?

Trees win for new and few-history customers on card fraud. The hybrid wins for regular customers on every seed (+0.023), and on AMLworld, where almost every account has history.

RQ4Which design choices transfer across domains?

TabM beats TabNet by +0.085 and +0.090. A fixed average is never beaten by a per-transaction gate. The value of graph information depends on the domain.

2Contributions and novelty

The novelty is in the evaluation design as much as in the model: one leak-checked evidence layer, given identically to trees and to neural models, separates what the information contributes from what the architecture contributes.

  1. A past-only evidence layer with explicit label delay. 87 features (entity memory, deviation, neighbour and evidence-signal features) computed from earlier rows only, with fraud labels admitted only after the delay. It is verified by a label-flip test, index checks and a shuffled-label null test (ROC-AUC 0.501 and 0.488, chance level).
  2. An information-controlled comparison of trees and neural networks. LightGBM+ and the neural hybrid read exactly the same layer. This isolates the architecture effect (−0.006 to +0.025 PR-AUC) from the information effect (+0.09 to +0.47), which is up to twenty times larger.
  3. A deviation model with delay-aware memory. Attention from the current transaction over the same customer's last 16 transactions. Each memory slot carries its label state (fraud, clean, or still pending under the delay), and an always-present empty slot handles first-time customers.
  4. Familiarity-stratified evaluation and calibration. Results are split by how well the customer is known (0, 1–5, 6+ earlier transactions). Familiarity-conditioned calibration (FCC) gives the hybrid the lowest calibration error (ECE 0.0019 against 0.0040 for LightGBM+).
  5. Transfer across domains with one adapter. The same code runs on real card fraud and on synthetic laundering. The sparsity penalty is rescaled by base-rate entropy, a rule that prevented feature-gate collapse when positives fall from 3.5% to 0.08%.
  6. Reported negative results. A per-transaction evidence gate, TabNet and a separate graph model are evaluated under the same protocol and reported even where they lose, with per-seed verdicts.

What this work does not claim

  • It does not beat the IEEE-CIS Kaggle-winning ensemble (0.946 ROC-AUC on Kaggle's own test file), which uses hundreds of engineered features and three boosting libraries.
  • It does not show neural models beating trees on card fraud; on IEEE-CIS they are slightly behind.
  • AMLworld is synthetic and covers ten active days, so its results test the method, not real-world laundering detection.

3Data and evaluation protocol

Both datasets are ordered in time and split chronologically, so every model is judged only on transactions that happen after everything it learned from.

IEEE-CIS. The Kaggle transaction and identity files are merged (590,540 rows; the Kaggle test file has no labels). No rows are removed. A customer stand-in, uid = card1 | addr1 | first-use day, is built only when all three parts exist; 10.5% of test rows have no uid. AMLworld HI-Small has real account numbers, so the customer is the sending bank and account. 1,108 rows from 11–18 September, about 59% laundering, are an end-of-simulation artefact and were removed.

Protocol. Seeds 42, 7 and 123 are fixed in advance. Each comparison draws 200 paired bootstrap resamples of the test set per seed. A difference is called better or worse only if its 95% interval excludes zero on every seed; otherwise the per-seed verdicts are listed. PR-AUC is the primary metric because positives are 3.5% and 0.12% of transactions.

4Method

One evidence layer feeds both neural component models and the LightGBM+ baseline. The two models score each transaction from different angles, and a fixed weighted average combines their logits.

4.1The evidence layer

Every feature is computed from earlier rows only. For entity memory, a value seen only a few times is pulled toward the overall fraud rate by smoothing, (frauds + 10 × base rate) ÷ (visible labels + 10), so one fraud on a new card does not read as a 100% fraud rate.

4.2Component models

TabM+G is a TabM-mini ensemble (Gorishniy et al., ICLR 2025): one 2-layer MLP of width 256 shared by 16 members. Each member has its own input scaling and output head. Numeric inputs use piecewise-linear encoding at training-set quantiles, which gives the network threshold-like steps similar to tree splits. A learned hard-concrete L0 gate per input column (Louizos et al., 2018) prunes uninformative features. Input: 505 numeric and 36 categorical columns.

The deviation model (+G) forms a 64-dimensional query from the current transaction's 19 core columns plus the deviation, entity-memory and neighbour features. It attends with 4 heads over the same customer's last 16 transactions. Each slot holds that transaction's columns, their difference from the current one, the time gap and the label state. A learned empty slot lets first-time customers attend to something.

Training. The training period is cut into 4 contiguous time blocks. Each block is scored by a model trained on the other three, giving out-of-fold scores for all 413,378 training rows. The combination weights are fitted on these, and test scores average the 4 fold models.

Combination. score = w₁·s₁ + w₂·s₂ + b on logits, with w₁ + w₂ = 1, both positive. TabM+G receives w₁ = 0.571, 0.487 and 0.499 on seeds 42, 7 and 123. Probabilities come from Platt scaling or from FCC, a logistic fit on the score, the customer's log transaction count and card age, and their interactions with the score.

The hybrid beats raw LightGBM by +0.084 PR-AUC on IEEE-CIS and +0.493 on AMLworld, every seed. Against LightGBM with the same evidence it is slightly behind on card fraud and ahead on money laundering.

Information drives the result; architecture choices move it by at most 0.02 PR-AUC, and only some of them are stable across seeds.

Finding. The gain is not explained by fresher labels: retraining raw LightGBM every 30 days on newly visible labels is worse than training once, on every seed (−0.026 on IEEE-CIS).

6.3TabM vs TabNet

TabNet selects a few features at each of 5 sequential steps with a sparse mask, so a feature it does not select contributes nothing at that step. Fraud evidence is spread across hundreds of weak C, D, V and identity columns plus the 87 evidence features. TabM-mini keeps every feature available to all 16 members and gives each member threshold-like steps through piecewise-linear encoding, so it combines many weak signals better. TabNet loses most on new customers (0.570 against 0.663), where those weak signals are all there is.

6.4Graph information: separate model or features

On card fraud the entity memory already stores each card's, address's and device's fraud rate over all users, so the cross-user signal is largely present before any graph is added. On laundering, neighbour relations encode fan-in and shared ownership directly, and the features help TabM+ (+0.011) and the hybrid (+0.010) on every seed. The separate graph model goes the opposite way on the two datasets: a small gain on IEEE-CIS (+0.0035, every seed) and a loss on AMLworld (−0.0055, every seed). Graph features in both models are the best placement on AMLworld and within 0.0035 of the best on IEEE-CIS, which is why the reported architecture uses them.

Model selection: the stop-split rule (highest stop PR-AUC, simpler on a tie) picks the no-graph hybrid on IEEE-CIS (0.7485 against 0.7476 on the stop split). On test the two tie (0.6300 against 0.6296). The stop split does not separate these variants on either dataset, so one architecture is reported for both and every variant is listed.

6.5Evidence gate vs fixed average

Why a smarter gate does not help:

  1. Both models score about 0.60 alone and read the same layer, so their rankings are highly correlated. Re-weighting only changes the few transactions where they disagree. The fixed weight moves between 0.49 and 0.57 across seeds while test PR-AUC stays at 0.629–0.631.
  2. The gate's inputs (how often the customer was seen, how old the uid is) are already inputs to the component models.
  3. A positive blend can only interpolate between the two scores; it cannot find a pattern neither model found.
  4. Weights are fitted on single-fold out-of-fold scores, while test scores average four folds and spread less. A per-transaction gate is more sensitive to this shift.
  5. About 520 parameters fitted on the earliest 70% of a drifting timeline, against 3 for the fixed average.

7Card fraud and money laundering compared

The evidence layer is the largest effect on both datasets, but it captures different things: delayed fraud labels on card fraud, account structure on laundering.

  1. Evidence. On IEEE-CIS the label-free layer adds nothing (−0.003); on AMLworld it adds +0.400, because counterparties, repeated pairs and velocity identify laundering accounts before any label is visible.
  2. Label delay matters much less where structure carries the signal: 0 to 30 days costs 0.18 PR-AUC on IEEE-CIS, while 0 to 2–3 days costs 0.02–0.03 on AMLworld.
  3. Neural vs trees. AMLworld favours the neural models in two ways. Real identities with long histories suit the deviation model, and with only 10 raw columns the tree advantages (sharp thresholds over many columns, missing-value signal, rare identifiers) matter less.
  4. Design choices that transfer: TabM over TabNet and a fixed average over the gate hold on both datasets. Graph placement does not transfer.

8Comparison with published work

On IEEE-CIS the split decides the score more than the model. This work is in the upper part of the out-of-time range on ROC-AUC, and its AMLworld F1 sits between the published graph-feature boosting results and the strongest multigraph GNNs.

9Limitations and threats to validity

  • Ensemble size. Each hybrid score averages 8 networks, while each LightGBM baseline is one model. Part of the hybrid's seed stability comes from this averaging; a cross-fitted LightGBM would make the comparison stricter.
  • Uncertainty. The bootstrap covers test-set sampling only; the three seeds cover training randomness. The intervals shown are per-seed intervals averaged over seeds, a summary rather than a confidence interval for the mean.
  • Model selection. The stop split cannot separate the graph variants (differences below 0.003). The reported architecture is the same on both datasets rather than the stop-split winner on IEEE-CIS; the two tie on test.
  • Customer proxy. IEEE-CIS has no real customer ID. The uid (card1, addr1, first-use day) is a proxy, and 10.5% of test rows have none.
  • Synthetic laundering. AMLworld is simulated with fixed typologies and spans ten active days. Some LightGBM ablations were run on seeds 42 and 123 only, and minority-class F1 is printed for seed 123 only.
  • Sparsity on AMLworld. Even with the rescaled penalty, TabM+'s feature gates close to 31–42% open, and the graph model's edge gates to 10–26%, so pruning remains strong on that dataset.

10Reproducibility

Every number on this page is printed by one of two notebooks or read from their stored per-seed result files. Code, notebooks and the full write-up are on GitHub: github.com/daiyanHasin/Fraud-Detection_IEEE-CIS_AMLWORLD.

Running. Download IEEE-CIS from the Kaggle competition (train_transaction.csv, train_identity.csv) and AMLworld HI-Small from the release accompanying Altman et al. (NeurIPS 2023). Set the data paths in the first cells and run each notebook once per seed, in the order 42, 7, 123. Every step caches its output and skips finished work. A GPU is strongly recommended; the AMLworld notebook uses LightGBM on GPU and mixed-precision neural training for its 5 million rows.
