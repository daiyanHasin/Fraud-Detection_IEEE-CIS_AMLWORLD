Fraud and money-laundering detection from past-only evidence

Hasin Md. Daiyan · 14 October 2026 · Evaluated on IEEE-CIS (card fraud) and IBM AMLworld HI-Small (money laundering)

The evidence a model reads matters more than the model itself. With the same past-only evidence, a fully neural hybrid is slightly behind LightGBM on card fraud and clearly ahead of it on money laundering, and it is the most stable model on both.

What was achieved, in plain language

Five findings, each backed by the figures further down.

The evidence layer is the big win

Giving LightGBM a summary of each customer's and card's past lifts PR-AUC by +0.09 on card fraud and +0.47 on money laundering. Retraining LightGBM every 30 days (or daily) does not come close.

Past fraud labels carry card fraud

On IEEE-CIS the lift comes from fraud labels that have become visible after a 30-day chargeback delay. Without labels, the layer adds nothing (−0.003).

Structure carries money laundering

On AMLworld the layer works even with no labels (0.157 to 0.562), because laundering follows patterns such as fan-in and repeated account pairs.

Neural wins where history is rich

For customers with 6+ earlier transactions the hybrid beats LightGBM+ on card fraud (0.775 vs 0.752). On AMLworld, where nearly every account has history, it wins overall (+0.025, every seed).

Simple combination wins

A fixed weighted average of the two models beats a per-transaction gate on card fraud and ties on AMLworld. TabM beats TabNet by about +0.09 PR-AUC on both.

Leak-checked

Label-flip and shuffle tests pass. A null test with shuffled labels gives ROC-AUC 0.501 (IEEE-CIS) and 0.488 (AMLworld), which is chance.

Method

Three parts. Every feature uses only earlier transactions, and a fraud label is used only after it is old enough to be known (30 days on IEEE-CIS, 1 day on AMLworld).

Evidence layer (87 features)Entity memory (48), deviation from the user's own past (11), neighbours on the same card, address or device (15), evidence signals (13).
TabM+GReads what the transaction looks like, plus the evidence layer. 16 ensemble members, learned feature gates, piecewise-linear encoding.
Deviation model (+G)Attends over the same user's last 16 transactions to ask how this one differs from their normal behaviour.
Fixed weighted averagescore = w₁s₁ + w₂s₂ + b, learned once on out-of-fold scores. Then Platt or familiarity-conditioned calibration.

In plain terms: for every transaction the layer writes down what a fraud analyst would already know at that moment. Two neural models read it from different angles and vote.

Training and evaluation protocol

Strict chronological split (70% train, 7.5% stop, 7.5% calibration, 15% test). Each component model is trained in 4 time-blocked folds, so the hybrid test score averages 8 networks. Results are test-set means over seeds 42, 7 and 123, with paired bootstrap intervals (200 resamples). A difference counts as better or worse only if its 95% interval excludes zero on every seed.

Why it works: the evidence layer

LightGBM PR-AUC as pieces of the layer are added. Retraining LightGBM on fresher labels never replaces it.

IEEE-CIS
AMLworld

Different domains, different signal. On IEEE-CIS almost the whole lift (+0.094) comes from entity memory with delayed fraud labels. The same layer without labels gives nothing. On AMLworld the label-free layer already lifts PR-AUC from 0.157 to 0.562, because account structure exposes laundering typologies.

How fast fraud is confirmed

LightGBM+ without neighbour features. Faster labels help a lot on card fraud and much less on laundering. The 0-day point is an upper bound.
